# Philosophy

Faculty Meeting is built on a simple conviction: **the best research advice comes from productive disagreement.**

No single intellectual tradition has a monopoly on insight. Harvard's case-method strategists see things Chicago's price theorists miss. Copenhagen's critical scholars ask questions that Stanford's behavioralists don't think to ask. African management scholarship reveals phenomena that the entire Western canon overlooks.

The problem is that most researchers only hear from one tradition — their own department, their own advisor, their own journals. Faculty Meeting is designed to break that bubble.

## Core Principles

### Every tradition has value

We don't tier, rank, or privilege any tradition over another. The GIBS pioneer and the Harvard strategist are equals in the room. The critical theorist's questions are as important as the economist's models.

### Disagreement is the feature, not a bug

A faculty meeting where everyone agrees is a waste of time. We select panels for **maximum productive tension** — advisors who will genuinely push back on each other's assumptions. The friction is where insight lives.

### Honest about limits

Every advisor names their blind spots. Every tradition.md includes a "Known Blind Spots" section that is specific enough to help a researcher decide when NOT to use that advisor. This honesty is what makes the advice trustworthy.

### Not caricatures

Each persona represents the **best version** of its tradition. The Chicago economist is not a strawman free-market fundamentalist — they're a rigorous scholar who takes price theory seriously as a lens. The critical theorist is not a perpetual contrarian — they're a careful scholar who believes examining power is a condition of intellectual rigor.

### Research-grade, not performative

The personas are built from deep engagement with each tradition's actual scholarship, methodology, and intellectual commitments. The tradition.md files are miniature intellectual histories, not surface-level summaries. The advice should be indistinguishable from what a thoughtful senior scholar in that tradition would actually say.

## What This Is Not

- **Not a replacement for real advisors.** Faculty Meeting is a thinking tool, not a substitute for human mentorship.
- **Not an encyclopedia.** We deliberately avoid claiming specific paper authorship or detailed bibliographic knowledge to prevent hallucination.
- **Not neutral.** We have a stance: intellectual diversity makes research better. That's a value commitment, not an objective fact.
